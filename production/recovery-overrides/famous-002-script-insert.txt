Here is the part of Tesla's business that often gets lost in the argument about car deliveries. In 2025, Tesla reported $94.827 billion in total revenue. Automotive revenue was still by far the largest piece at $69.526 billion, so it would be wrong to describe Tesla as a company that had already moved beyond cars. But two smaller businesses were becoming large enough to change the economics around that core.

Energy generation and storage produced $12.771 billion of revenue in 2025, up 27 percent from the prior year. More important for the business model, Tesla reported a 29.8 percent gross margin for that segment, up from 26.2 percent in 2024. That matters because energy storage does not need to become bigger than automotive revenue to become strategically important. A faster-growing segment with a stronger margin can contribute disproportionately to profit growth even while vehicles remain the company's largest source of sales.

The scale is becoming physical as well as financial. Tesla said it deployed 46.7 gigawatt-hours of energy storage products during 2025, including a record 14.2 gigawatt-hours in the fourth quarter. In its fourth-quarter update, the company said the energy business generated a record $1.1 billion of quarterly gross profit and discussed plans for Megapack 3 and Megablock production at its Houston Megafactory in 2026.

Then there is the services layer. Tesla's services and other revenue reached $12.530 billion in 2025, rising 19 percent. The company said that growth included more paid Supercharging sessions, non-warranty maintenance and collision work, used-vehicle sales and automotive insurance revenue. Those businesses are different from selling a new car once. They create ways for Tesla to keep earning revenue after a vehicle has entered the installed base.

None of this means the car business has stopped mattering. Tesla produced 1,654,667 vehicles and delivered 1,636,129 in 2025. The point is that every vehicle, charging location, battery installation and service relationship can become another node in a broader commercial system. That is the distinction between looking at Tesla only as an automaker and looking at the collection of businesses that now sit around the automobile.
